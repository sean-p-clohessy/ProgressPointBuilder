# Report logic

## Authority and category groupings

The official progress indicator controls the report’s overall judgement. It must never be replaced by an average of category ratings.

- Core judgements: behaviour and attitude, engagement in learning, and effort and application
- Framework context: the official progress indicator remains authoritative
- Optional supporting facts: attendance, punctuality, context, evidence, and an agreed next step

## Profile analysis

The engine identifies the strongest and weakest of the three core judgements, meaningful contrasts with the official indicator, supplied percentages, and context modifiers. Mixed ratings are valid evidence, not validation failures.

## Narrative patterns

Reports contain an opening aligned to the official indicator, selected strengths, participation evidence, a main development area, and an actionable conclusion. Sentence selection is prioritised so that concise reports do not become a category-by-category list.

## Contrast detection

Explicit rules cover strong effort with weak outcomes, good current work with poor attendance, medical absence, late enrolment, behaviour concerns, and different practical and written performance.

## Deterministic selection

Phrase choice is derived from stable keys and an explicit variation index. Identical inputs and variation index yield identical text. Changing the variation index may change phrasing but never facts or judgement.

## Context rules

Structured context types will use controlled, sensitive wording. Short evidence fields may be inserted into known sentence templates with only safe punctuation and capitalisation handling. Raw notes will not be rewritten or silently inserted.

## Known limitations

The application does not interpret free text, infer circumstances, or make professional judgements. Lecturer review and personalisation remain essential.
